None of that is settled yet, so these KPIs are not tailored to any individual. We will settle each profile in person over the next few days, rather than make abstract assumptions about people. Those planning to raise soon will go first. What stands in the meantime are KPIs of minimal engagement.

TL;DR

The daily order. Begin with one quote post in the morning: a genuine disagreement or useful follow-up. Send three specific DMs—one warm, one peer-level and one upward. Leave five replies: three on recent, quiet posts and two early in busy threads; then follow three of the smaller accounts you replied to. Publish two original posts later: one useful and one open-ended, with one in the afternoon and one in the evening.

Every day · monitored

Daily KPIs

Their intention is habituation: to make Twitter people out of us. They are to be completed every single day, and they will be monitored. We are now residents of Silicon Valley; Twitter is the platform to engage in here, and the point of these KPIs is to enable us to continuously think in terms of Twitter clout.

One

Quote post

Quote-tweet something you genuinely disagree with and say why in your own words. Or have something to follow up with.

Try to also '@' someone with 500 to 3,000 followers if the point you are making would be relevant to them.

When and why. Do this in the morning while you scroll. Scroll actively. Disagreement here is ideal coz you'll likely get a reply.

Two

Original posts

Two posts, different in kind.

The useful one. Something they can actually use: a method, a template, a checklist, a screenshot of how you do it.

The open one. Short, plain text, built to be replied to: a question, a claim someone can push against, something left unfinished.

Try to put images/videos.

When and why. One in the afternoon, one in the evening. Don't do more than two or three a day, as your own posts compete for space in people's feeds.

Five

Replies

Three on quiet posts. Few replies so far, recent, from accounts a bit bigger than you.

Two on busy threads. Get in early, before the pile builds.

Why. On a quiet post you are most of the conversation, so the author reads you and may reply. On a busy thread you are writing for the other readers, so only something new gets seen.

Three

Deliberate follows

Follow the small accounts you replied to today.

Why. This is what turns a reply into a mutual. Once someone follows you back, X weights their predicted likelihood of replying to one of your original posts at 20 instead of 5 — roughly 4×. It does not boost your replies or reposts.

General guidelines

i · Timing

Post into attention that already exists

Use live attention, launch windows and the words people are already searching for. Keep drafts ready so the moment does not depend on spontaneity.

Live window

Post while the event is happening

During a conference, launch or announcement, attention piles onto a few search terms. Post inside that window. Tie it back to your own subject rather than just reporting.

Search language

Use the words the conversation has settled on

Whether people find you depends on the terms you use. If everyone calls something by a particular name, use that name.

Why. The words in the post decide which searches it can enter.
Daily practice

Take inventory

Keep a running repository of your takes on what you see in your space. The moment you have one, put it in drafts. When a window opens, you already have something to post.

Then. Let the strongest takes grow into essays or projects.
ii · Access

Turn where you are into material

Use the places, rooms and details available to you as particulars that make a broader thesis concrete.

Scarcity

Post when you get somewhere most people cannot

Invites, closed gatherings, selective events, private rooms. Access is interesting in itself. Frame what you saw through your own subject.

Daily practice

Build your Particulars List

Keep a growing repository of specific things in the world that help you make a point about your thesis. Move from the universal claim to the particular thing in front of you.

The test. Make a real connection to what you are building, not a forced one.
iii · Other people

Borrow audiences you have not built

Give collaborators, relevant accounts and people you meet a real reason to respond, repost or continue the relationship.

Collaboration

Post about working with someone, not standing near them

If you actually collaborated, they have a reason to repost you because the post is about their work too.

Useful notification

Tag the specific people it is useful to

Try accounts with 500 to 3,000 followers whose work the post actually bears on. It lands in their notifications and gives them a reason to share it.

Keep it specific. The point should genuinely be relevant to them.
In person

Ask for the follow directly

When you meet someone in person, ask them to follow you. Say it plainly. Offering to follow them back weakens the ask and usually goes nowhere.

Relationship practice

Build the Hitlist as a mountain

Start with people you can reach now: those who reply often, have smaller followings or have met you. Each real relationship makes the next tier more plausible.

Climb. A reply or follow from someone established makes the people around them more likely to answer you.
iv · Your work

Publish continuously

Turn the work already happening into credible material, make it useful, and vary the subject so your own posts do not crowd one another out.

Unfinished work · example one

Post your work while it is unfinished

Prototypes, rough footage, drafts, things that do not work yet. The work already exists, and showing it makes everything else you say credible.

Reusable value

Post things a reader can use

Prompts, methods, workflows. Anything reusable turns readers into followers more reliably than opinion because they want the next one.

Feed spacing

Vary your subject matter across the day

Same-topic posts get attenuated through a separate mechanism that tracks the subject. Three posts on one theme compete twice over.

Also. Keep your own daily output to two or three posts.
Progress practice

Keep Milestones

Record what you have achieved on the way: a stage you have moved past, the level you are on now, or the target you are working toward.

Why. Visible progress separates people doing the work from people talking about it.
Discipline

Keep the programme running

This part is practice, not illustration. Set the boundary once, then review the experiment every week.

Keep a Constraints List

Decide what you will not post. The purpose is that no current or potential stakeholder — anyone who might give money, introductions or another resource — opts out because of something on your timeline. Treat it as your own PR training.

Review the week every Sunday

Go through your analytics, traction and stats. Record what worked and what did not. The programme is experimental; the review is how one person's test becomes a usable head start for everyone else.

Complete the KPIs every day. Review them every Sunday.
